This track assumes the foundations:
[Start here](/start-here/), [Seeing what Nix sees](/seeing-what-nix-sees/)
and [Reading errors and tracing](/reading-errors/).

Here Nix runs **next to** your distribution. It keeps everything it builds in
`/nix/store` and leaves your distribution's packages alone; your distribution
still manages the system. What Nix gives you on top:

- **Dev environments** per project, with exactly the tools that project needs.
- **Packages for your user**, with history and rollback.
- **Your home configuration** (dotfiles, programs) with home-manager.

All of them start from a `flake.nix`.

<Aside>
  Nix also runs on macOS and on ARM machines, and the commands are the same.
  The examples on this site are only run and checked on x86_64 Linux.
</Aside>
